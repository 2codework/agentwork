[Opening]
Here is a simple way to compare two paper-plane designs without turning a
small experiment into a confusing guessing game. We are not trying to prove
which plane is best for everyone. We are only comparing our two designs
under the same conditions in one practice session.

[Fact F1]
In this practice test, both planes use one sheet of the same size and type
of paper. Only the fold design changes.

[Extra explanation]
You can name the designs A and B. Write the names on a separate record sheet,
not on the planes. Clear a safe indoor space and keep people out of the
flight path. Use the same starting line for every throw.

[Fact F2]
Each design gets three throws, so this practice session has six throws in total.

[Extra explanation]
Take turns between A and B rather than finishing one design first. This is
a small practice exercise, not a competition. A surprising result is still
worth writing down, and one long flight does not tell the whole story.

[Fact F3]
Record all six distances, including short flights. Do not discard a result
just because it looks disappointing.

[Ending]
Keep the record with the two design names. Now another person can see what
you actually measured, rather than only hearing which plane you liked.
